# Community mapping

Why: posting in the wrong group, or breaking its rules, gets the account banned and wastes effort. The audience we want is business owners, not homeowners.

## Steps
1. **Search** public sources: web search, the platform's public pages, org sites, Meetup, podcasts. Keep the evidence for each candidate.
2. **Capture for each community:** name, URL, size, public or private, activity (recent post dates or meetup cadence), promo rules (promo thread or day, no ads, no AI content, owners only, question-only posts), why it fits, and the source with its retrieval date.
3. **Mark any unverified field** as "not verified". Never list a URL you didn't confirm. Label off-platform counts (Meetup, LinkedIn) as such, not as group size.
4. **Rank by business-owner fit:** owner-operator trade groups in the target area first, then national supplier-run trade groups, then general networking. Exclude homeowner groups.
5. **Never invent groups or numbers.** A short honest list beats a padded one, so state any shortfall.
6. **Facebook:** most fields need a logged-in check. Hand the unverified fields to whichever teammate's browser is signed in, rather than guessing or signing in yourself.
7. **AI-content and pitching bans:** flag them. In those communities the owner writes or rewrites posts personally, and we join as a peer.
8. **Audience questions and objections** you see in threads go into the team handoffs file as blog ideas, each with the question, the community, a link, and the date.
9. **Save** to the research folder as <platform>-<area>-YYYY-MM-DD.md with Method and Limitations sections, then notify the PM.

Skills to reference: `gw-lead-generation` (awareness levels, top-20% traits, magnet-problem discovery, collect FAQs and objections, find local trade events and groups).
