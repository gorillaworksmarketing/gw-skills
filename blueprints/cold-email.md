## Lead research (GW Lead Research; skill `cold-email-lead-research`)
**Sources (free only):** Google Maps and WA business directories (look up, never bulk-scrape), company websites and About pages, GBP and public reviews, public LinkedIn professional content, local news, podcasts and talks, trade associations and chamber lists, public permit data. Never log into private accounts or scrape behind logins.
**Signals** [T1][T11][CH][V5]: new location or service area, new permits, hiring installers, new owner or new role, a recent public post on the topic, recent projects posted, few reviews or a review surge, no ads, a weak or missing website (classify: no site, social only, weak site, solid site). Pair every prospect with one dated signal.
**One niche per list** [V7]: a fence list and a roofing list are separate campaigns; never mix trades (they care about different problems).
**Tight filters** [V5]: find the real break points from current clients (crew size, service area, role, stated focus) and run the cheapest, highest-priority filter first ("golden ICP" waterfall). Vary the message by which signals are true.
**Map the TAM** [V6]: list the whole addressable market per niche and sub-type before testing; size daily volume so a list is not burned faster than it can be re-used.
**Email finding:** first@, first.last@, flast@ on the company domain, then verify with free checkers (Bulk Email Checker, MxToolbox). Flag catch-all domains. Keep bounces under 2%. Re-verify anything older than 7 days before a send [CH].
**Lookalike lists** [T12][V4]: start from the best customers (Real Great Roofing) and from the traits of positive repliers; find similar WA fencing and roofing firms by review count, size, ads, hiring, permits.
**3-layer research** [T25] (target 5-7 minutes): human (role, career path, public talks or posts), company (site, press, values, projects, our own history with them), space (their vocabulary: jobs, estimates, crews, leads).
**Account brief** [CH prospecting]: fit, signal (dated), priority (hot, warm, cold, skip), 3-5 verified hooks with source URL and date and confidence, angle suggestion, what not to mention, and "hook found: yes or no". Facts only; the copywriter picks the angle.
**Hold the lead if there is no strong hook.** Quality over quantity (for example 20 deeply researched emails a week).
**Personalization don'ts** [T25]: cheers with no tie; "saw your profile"; generic subjects; a pitch in the preview; private-feeling items; sensitive topics (health, family tragedy, religion, politics, finances, age, income).
**CRM fields:** name, company, role, city, trade, website, email, verify status and date, signal and date, hook 1-3, source URL 1-3, confidence, notes, offer arm (A-D), problem used, campaign and last campaign date, touch number, last touch, next touch, reply type, positive reply time, reply-to-response minutes, call booked (y/n), call held, nurture date, status, utm_campaign, unsubscribe flag, hold reason, contact source URL and date (keep for CAN-SPAM questions), **Reviews** (up to 3 qualifying review lines or NO QUALIFYING REVIEW; Nov 13).

## Roofing landing-page email (CURRENT for every roofing lead; spec v1 Nov 10 2026, Brennan approved for the skills Nov 11 2026, 1:35 PM PT)
Source: /workspace/roofing-rewrite-2026-11-10/format-spec.md (shared box), copied here so bots can read it at a stable path. Skills: `cold-email-copy`, `cold-email-editing`, `gw-qa-gate` 7c.
**Goal:** every email has one job: get the owner to click through to the personal landing page built for their company. No call booking, no "reply yes", no offer dollar amounts.
**HTML is Brennan's.** He builds and owns the template in Ember. Don't put the HTML template in Notion, the API guide, or anywhere else. Bots write only the plain-text copy slots. "Hi {{first_name}}", the "Inside your page" checklist, the 3-channel strip, the signature, and the footer/opt-out are fixed in Ember; bots never write them.
**Where the copy goes:** Notion property **Email Copy** (the shared copy column) in Cold Leads – Roofing. Plain text, one labeled line per slot, in this order and spelled exactly:
```
Subject: ...
Preview: ...
Eyebrow: <Review Count> Google reviews at <Rating, 1 decimal>
Headline: ...
Review quote: ...
Review name: ...
Body paragraph 1: ...
Body paragraph 2: ...
Proof line: Real Great Roofing: "$90,000 in leads from just one mail drop."
CTA: See my page -> <Prospect Page URL, or {{page_link}} if empty>
```
**Ember slot mapping:** Preview = {{preview_text}}; Eyebrow = {{review_count}} + {{rating}} (also the Review Count and Rating properties); Headline = {{headline}}; Review quote = {{review_quote}}; Review name = {{review_name}}; Body paragraph 1/2 = the 2 body paragraphs; Proof line = {{proof_line}}; CTA = button to {{page_link}}. The editor puts the final version in **Edited Email Copy**, same labeled layout.
**Slot rules:**
1. **Subject:** short, with the company's name, for example "Built a page for Titan Roofing". No fake Re:, no urgency, no pitch words.
2. **Preview:** one line, under 90 characters, about what's on the page. Default: "Your Google reviews, a sample mailer and the homes near your recent roofs."
3. **Headline:** 3 to 8 words, specific to this company, taken from a verified hook (an award, warranty, years in business, service area, owner name).
4. **Review quote:** a real Google review of this company, copied word for word. **From Nov 13 it comes from a line in the row's Reviews field** (Pipeline rules, rule 1). Before Nov 13 it could come from the row (Reviews), its page body, or the company's own site where it's clearly labeled as a Google review (for example "Google Review", "Posted on Google", or Trustindex "source of the review is Google"). Shorten only with "..."; never reword, fix typos, or merge two reviews. Aim for 20 words or fewer. Skip any review that mentions price, cost, or money. **Nov 11 approval:** 5 stars and about 6 months old or newer, with proof of the stars and the date. No usable Google review: don't write the email; hold the row and say why.
5. **Review name:** exactly as the source shows it ("Sarah M.", "Isabel").
6. **Body paragraph 1:** 25 to 40 words. One or two verified company details (services, towns, crew, owner, award, warranty), then a line that ties it to the nearby homes that still need the same work.
7. **Body paragraph 2:** "I built <short company name> a personal page showing how cold email, custom direct mail and a landing page for every homeowner reach the homes around your recent jobs while your crew's work is still the talk of the street."
8. **Proof line:** always exactly `Real Great Roofing: "$90,000 in leads from just one mail drop."` One client's result. No campaign details, no other stats. It is the approved proof quote and the only dollar figure allowed; keep it verbatim.
9. **CTA:** `See my page -> ` + the row's Prospect Page URL if one is set, otherwise `{{page_link}}`. Never invent a URL.
**Hard rules:** no price or cost wording at all (no $0.78, no "under $1", no stamp comparisons, no "$750 value" or "$500 off"); the only dollar figure is the proof line. Never write "thousands of other customers" (not an approved claim; Nov 11). Nothing invented: each row gets a `gw-fact-check` claim table covering the count and rating, the quote and its source, every company fact, and the proof line. No hashtags, no em dashes in our own words, no google.com/url wrappers, no AI tells; 7th-grade reading level. Draft only: never set Status = Send, never send, never change Ember send settings. The copywriter sets Editing as usual; if the editor is holding a row, keep its Status; don't touch Copywriter, research, assignment, stage, or approval fields. Run the coherence check before edit and QA: every slot makes sense read in template order.
**Editor checklist:** open the quote's source and confirm it matches word for word and is labeled as a Google review; confirm the reviewer name; confirm Eyebrow matches Review Count and Rating or the live Maps values in the Reviews capture (note any mismatch; Nov 13); confirm the screenshot's listing name matches the company (Nov 13); confirm each company fact against its source; confirm the proof line is exact and there's no other dollar amount or price word; confirm all labeled lines are present, in order, with nothing extra.
**Old rules replaced for roofing (Nov 11 2026):** no link in email 1 (the CTA links to the page); the 15-minute call ask and the "reply yes" claim; the opt-out line and postal address written in the copy (CAN-SPAM still applies: Ember's fixed footer carries the opt-out and the Kirkland address; the editor flags Brennan if that footer isn't confirmed); "never name the deliverable" (the email names the personal page); offer arms and offer values. The Real Great Roofing "one client's result" rule stays.
**Open (ask Brennan):** roofing follow-up touches and positive-reply text (the spec covers email 1 only); whether the CTA page URL needs UTM tags and a link-log row; whether other trades move to this format.

## Pipeline rules: rows never stall (Brennan, Nov 13 2026, 2:13 PM PT)

## Offer test (FIRST test; Brennan, Oct 30 2026)
**SUPERSEDED for roofing on Nov 11, 2026** (Brennan approved the landing-page format at 1:35 PM PT): roofing cold email no longer uses the four offer arms, offer values, or the 15-minute claim call. Kept below as history and for any other trade until Brennan says otherwise.
**Flow [V7]:** a tangible free offer, the prospect replies "yes", then they claim it by booking a **15-minute call**. The call fulfills the offer and becomes discovery (Brennan runs it; opener in `gw-lead-generation`).
**Arms (wording exact; do not edit):**
- **A)** Free landing page for direct mail ($750 value) + up to $500 in free spend
- **B)** Free page on your website specific to direct mail ($750 value) + up to $500 off
- **C)** Up to $500 off first campaign
- **D)** Build a free targeted lead list ($500 value)
Note: A says "free spend", B says "off". Keep each exactly as Brennan wrote it unless he changes it. The $750 and $500 values are his figures; use them only inside these arms.
**Why offers first [V7][V5][V6]:** the offer moves results most; copy and subject tests come after a winning offer exists.
**Design:**
1. One niche per test list (fencing first). Assign leads to arms at random; one arm per lead for the whole sequence.
2. Hold everything else constant: template, subject style, CTA, cadence, send days, mailboxes (rotate arms across mailboxes evenly).
3. Primary metric: **booked 15-minute calls per 1,000 sends**. Secondary: positive ("yes") replies per human reply. Guardrails: bounces, complaints, unsubscribes. Reply rate alone is a vanity metric [V7].
4. Judge after about 1,000 sends per arm; never call a winner on a few hundred [V7]. Capacity estimate at the hold rate (2 mailboxes x about 30 a day = about 60 a day): 4 arms x 1,000 = about 67 sending days. Options: run two arms at a time, or add warmed mailboxes (Brennan decides).
5. After a winner: scale it, then test within it (problem, angle, CTA, subject); split volume 70% proven / 20% iterations / 10% new offers [V6].
6. Segment campaigns by **problem + offer** [V7]. No-reply leads can get a new problem + offer about a month later.
**Removed:** the earlier front-end offers (sample postcard, list preview, mail teardown, 90-second video) were never approved and are not used.

## Metrics and testing

Decision metrics: booked calls per 1,000 sends and positive reply rate. Email-to-lead ratios: sends per positive reply, per booked call, per client. Attribute bookings and clients back to the send log by arm and problem; most positive replies is not always most meetings [V6]. Guardrails: bounces, unsubscribes, complaints.
