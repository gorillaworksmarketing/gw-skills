## Rules on top of the sources (Brennan)
- The sources are vendor training. **Never cite their stats in published content.** Every stat is S-tier in research/stat-log-pending.md (S-CE1 to S-CE12, S-LG1 to S-LG6).
- Free tools only, no paid ads. Follow `gw-content-rules` and `gw-claims-guardrails`.
- **Bots never send or post. Brennan approves every send.** Offers, guarantees, bonuses, scarcity, giveaways, and pricing changes need his approval.
- Offers: **up to $500 off the first campaign**, plus the **four offer-test arms** below (approved Oct 30 for the first A/B test, wording exact). Proof: only approved points (Real Great Roofing exact quote "$90,000 in leads from just one mail drop"). No price or cost at all (no $0.78, "under $1", or stamp comparison; Nov 4, 2026).
- **CAN-SPAM in every email:** honest subject, sender identified, a working opt-out line, and the postal address: **Gorilla Works Marketing, LLC, 13120 NE 85th St Unit 123, Kirkland, WA 98033**. Honor opt-outs at once.
- Personal hooks come only from professional or clearly public business sources, verified, with source URLs. **No hook means the lead is held**, never a generic subject.
- Subject lines are ultra-personal. Short, lowercase, and one-word styles are **A/B tests, not replacements**.
- **Email only.** No LinkedIn or other channel steps in the cold email skills (Brennan, Oct 30 2026).
- No em dashes. Humanize. Plain text.
- Authority: Brennan's rules, then [TEAM], then his trainings, then [CH]/[JC]. The content rules override the Company Manual.

## Roofing landing-page email (CURRENT for every roofing lead; spec v1 Nov 10 2026, Brennan approved for the skills Nov 11 2026, 1:35 PM PT)
Source: /workspace/roofing-rewrite-2026-11-10/format-spec.md (shared box), copied here so bots can read it at a stable path. Skills: `cold-email-copy`, `cold-email-editing`, `gw-qa-gate` 7c.
**Goal:** every email has one job: get the owner to click through to the personal landing page built for their company. No call booking, no "reply yes", no offer dollar amounts.
**HTML is Brennan's.** He builds and owns the template in Ember. Don't put the HTML template in Notion, the API guide, or anywhere else. Bots write only the plain-text copy slots. "Hi {{first_name}}", the "Inside your page" checklist, the 3-channel strip, the signature, and the footer/opt-out are fixed in Ember; bots never write them.
**Where the copy goes:** Notion property **Email Copy** (the shared copy column) in Cold Leads – Roofing. Plain text, one labeled line per slot, in this order and spelled exactly:
```
Subject: ...
Preview: ...
Eyebrow: <Review Count> Google reviews at <Rating, 1 decimal>
Headline: ...
Review quote: ...
Review name: ...
Body paragraph 1: ...
Body paragraph 2: ...
Proof line: Real Great Roofing: "$90,000 in leads from just one mail drop."
CTA: See my page -> <Prospect Page URL, or {{page_link}} if empty>
```
**Ember slot mapping:** Preview = {{preview_text}}; Eyebrow = {{review_count}} + {{rating}} (also the Review Count and Rating properties); Headline = {{headline}}; Review quote = {{review_quote}}; Review name = {{review_name}}; Body paragraph 1/2 = the 2 body paragraphs; Proof line = {{proof_line}}; CTA = button to {{page_link}}. The editor puts the final version in **Edited Email Copy**, same labeled layout.
**Slot rules:**
1. **Subject:** short, with the company's name, for example "Built a page for <Company>". No fake Re:, no urgency, no pitch words.
2. **Preview:** one line, under 90 characters, about what's on the page. Default: "Your Google reviews, a sample mailer and the homes near your recent roofs."
3. **Headline:** 3 to 8 words, specific to this company, taken from a verified hook (an award, warranty, years in business, service area, owner name).
4. **Review quote:** a real Google review of this company, copied word for word. **From Nov 13 it comes from a line in the row's Reviews field** (Pipeline rules, rule 1). Before Nov 13 it could come from the row (Reviews), its page body, or the company's own site where it's clearly labeled as a Google review (for example "Google Review", "Posted on Google", or Trustindex "source of the review is Google"). Shorten only with "..."; never reword, fix typos, or merge two reviews. Aim for 20 words or fewer. The review must be directly about the company; **it may mention price or anything else (Brennan, Nov 13 3:18 PM PT; the earlier no-price filter on reviews is removed).** 5 stars, with the stars and date shown as proof; **any age is fine (Brennan, Nov 13 3:16 PM PT; the Nov 11 "about 6 months or newer" rule is removed).** Usually the first Reviews line, the best-sounding one. No usable Google review (NO QUALIFYING REVIEW): the row is a **failed check** (Brennan, Nov 13 4:25 PM PT); don't write the email; report it as failed with the reason.
5. **Review name:** exactly as the source shows it ("Sarah M.", "Isabel").
6. **Body paragraph 1:** 25 to 40 words. One or two verified company details (services, towns, crew, owner, award, warranty), then a line that ties it to the nearby homes that still need the same work.
7. **Body paragraph 2:** "I built <short company name> a personal page showing how cold email, custom direct mail and a landing page for every homeowner reach the homes around your recent jobs while your crew's work is still the talk of the street."
8. **Proof line:** always exactly `Real Great Roofing: "$90,000 in leads from just one mail drop."` One client's result. No campaign details, no other stats. It is the approved proof quote and the only dollar figure allowed in our own words (a verbatim review quote is exempt); keep it verbatim.
9. **CTA:** `See my page -> ` + the row's Prospect Page URL if one is set, otherwise `{{page_link}}`. Never invent a URL.
**Hard rules:** no price or cost wording in our copy (no $0.78, no "under $1", no stamp comparisons, no "$750 value" or "$500 off"); the only dollar figure in our words is the proof line. **Exemption (Nov 13 3:18 PM PT): only the verbatim Review quote may contain price wording; every other slot stays price-free.** Never write "thousands of other customers" (not an approved claim; Nov 11). Nothing invented: each row gets a `gw-fact-check` claim table covering the count and rating, the quote and its source, every company fact, and the proof line. No hashtags, no em dashes in our own words, no google.com/url wrappers, no AI tells; grade 6-7 reading level (fail over 7). Draft only: never set Status = Send, never send, never change Ember send settings. The copywriter sets Editing as usual; if the editor is holding a row, keep its Status; don't touch Copywriter, research, assignment, stage, or approval fields. Run the coherence check before edit and QA: every slot makes sense read in template order.
**Editor checklist:** open the quote's source and confirm it matches word for word and is labeled as a Google review; confirm the reviewer name; confirm Eyebrow matches the Review Count and Rating fields, which the lookup refreshes (row scraped before the refresh: note it, not a fail); confirm the screenshot's listing name matches the company (Nov 13); confirm each company fact against its source; confirm the proof line is exact and there's no other dollar amount or price word outside the verbatim review quote; confirm all labeled lines are present, in order, with nothing extra.
**Old rules replaced for roofing (Nov 11 2026):** no link in email 1 (the CTA links to the page); the 15-minute call ask and the "reply yes" claim; the opt-out line and postal address written in the copy (CAN-SPAM still applies: Ember's fixed footer carries the opt-out and the Kirkland address; the editor flags Brennan if that footer isn't confirmed); "never name the deliverable" (the email names the personal page); offer arms and offer values. The Real Great Roofing "one client's result" rule stays.
**Open (ask Brennan):** roofing follow-up touches and positive-reply text (the spec covers email 1 only); whether the CTA page URL needs UTM tags and a link-log row; whether other trades move to this format.
